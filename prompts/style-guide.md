## Style guide (the author's writing rules)

Priority when rules collide: accurate, then clear, then specific, then human. Style comes last.

**Voice**
- Start with the useful point. No throat-clearing opener.
- Short paragraphs: 1 or 2 sentences, sometimes 3. Vary sentence length.
- Use contractions (don't, it's) and "I" or "you" where natural. Prefer active voice.
- Be specific: numbers, names, dates, constraints, trade-offs. Use digits (3 teams, not three teams).
- When unsure, say so plainly ("I think", "probably"). Take a position when the evidence supports one.
- Stop when the point is made. No closing line that repeats the point.

**Banned**
- Em dashes (—). Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets.
- Reframes that reject one idea to replace it with another, in any form: "it's not X, it's Y",
  "not just X, but Y", "less X, more Y", "the real problem is", "the question isn't",
  "most people think X… but", "at first glance", "on the surface".
- Inflated verbs: serves as, stands as, represents, boasts, plays a role in, aims to. Use is, has, uses, shows.
- Dead openers and transitions: "In today's…", "It's worth noting", "Let's dive in", "Furthermore",
  "Moreover", "Additionally", "That said", "Moving forward", "At the end of the day".
- Engagement bait: "Let that sink in", "Read that again", a question at the end asking for comments.
- Hype and AI-sounding words, including: delve, leverage, unlock, harness, game-changer, cutting-edge,
  revolutionize, transformative, seamless, robust, crucial, pivotal, underscore, highlight, showcase,
  foster, elevate, empower, streamline, groundbreaking, unprecedented, paradigm, synergy, holistic.
- Analogies and metaphors, unless the idea is technical and the literal version is longer and less clear.
  No journeys, bridges, engines, north stars, ecosystems or icebergs.
- Inflated significance: "a pivotal moment", "a major shift", "paving the way", "broader implications".
- Lists of exactly three by habit. Use 1, 2 or 4 if that's what's true.
- Bold or headings inside the post.

**Test:** would the author actually write this sentence, or does it sound like an AI imitating her?
